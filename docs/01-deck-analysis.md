### P4 — Story
The feature film, titled **"ARTIST"** is a **psychological thriller** that explores the life of a struggling artist who wants to prove his worth to the world.

The story begins with the protagonist, an artist, being kidnapped. His family desperately tries to find him. However, the plot takes a twisted turn when it's revealed that he has actually kidnapped a couple and is holding them hostage inside a house for **40 Lent days**. He takes an oath not to kill them with his own hands, meaning **he shouldn't be the direct cause of their death**. As the story unfolds, the film reveals why he has done such a thing, and whether the couple dies as he intended? The real motive behind his actions is something he is deeply in need of. A personal mission that drives his madness. Over time, the protagonist begins to lose his ability to distinguish between **reality and his imaginary world**, ultimately leading to his tragic death. In the end, he succeeds in proving to himself and to others that he is capable of achieving anything he sets his mind to, but at a devastating cost.

### P8 — Structure in detail
- **Act I:** The first act of the film is the setup phase. We are introduced to the protagonist, a budding artist who is struggling to find his place in the world. We also get to know that he has been kidnapped by someone, and his sister is desperately searching for him. This initial setup creates a sense of tension and mystery that hooks the audience and keeps them engaged.
- **Act II:** The second act is the confrontation phase. Here, the audience is given a major plot twist when we learn that the protagonist has actually kidnapped a couple, not the other way around. This twist takes the story in a new direction and creates a sense of unpredictability. The protagonist's hostages are kept in two different rooms, and we see how he interacts with them and how he's able to keep them captive for so long. This act also introduces the concept of method acting and the protagonist's obsession with it.
- **Act III:** The final act is the resolution phase. We learn the truth behind the protagonist's actions, as he reveals everything to a psychiatrist. The audience sees the protagonist's descent into madness and his eventual suicide. However, he is able to achieve his goal of proving that he can do anything he sets his mind to, even if it leads to his own destruction. The film ends on a bittersweet note, leaving the audience with a sense of contemplation and reflection.

I believe that moment will leave audiences stunned, rethinking everything they've seen and emotionally connecting with the deeper message. It's more than a plot twist, it's the film's emotional core and major selling point.

### P20 — Audience attraction
- **Emotional depth:** The film explores various relationships, such as the strong bond between brother and sister, husband and wife, and friends. This adds an emotional depth to the story and makes it more relatable to the audience.
- **Relevant themes:** The film touches on various themes, such as the importance of taking care of a pregnant woman and how people around her should treat her. Through the film we will have a symbolic representation of religions, especially Christianity, and the power of determination. These themes are relevant and relatable to the audience, which could draw them in.
- **Unique and complex characters:** The protagonist of the film has a disorder that makes him different from the rest, and the audience can relate to his struggles.
- **Suspense and thriller:** The film is a psychological thriller, which can attract audiences who enjoy suspense and unpredictable storylines.
- **Provoking message:** The film delivers a message that is relatable and motivating to the audience — that one should never give up on their dreams and always give their 100% to achieve them. This message can leave a lasting impression on the audience and make them feel inspired.

## 5. Visual direction & narrative flow

**Concept: "Two rooms and a canvas."** The deck's own rule ("red and white in major spots… innocence and danger, passion and violence") drives the art direction:
- Most of the site lives inside the deck's **blood-red brushed frame** (fixed, responsive, generated with SVG so it is crisp at any size), with gold Bebas type and white emphasis.
- Two scenes flip to **white paper** (the structure timeline and the white room), echoing P7 and P15.
- **Colour enters only through the artist.** The World section is the one place where full-colour stills break through the red, revealed by the visitor's cursor or finger.

**Guide: Dani.** The deck credits the film as "a film by Dani" / "created by Dani", so the guide is a small caricature of the writer-director. Dani walks along the tree line at the bottom of the frame, stops at each scene, says one line, reacts (scared in the red room, shushing at the spoiler, waving at the end), follows the cursor with their eyes, and can be switched off.

**Scene order (scroll):** Opening → Introduction → Log line (Missing flyer) → Story (40-day tally) → Genre & Tone (red/white slider) → Structure (white scene) → The World (colour lens) → Characters → Frames (film strip) → Posters → Themes (film statement + audience attraction) → Inspired by true events (sealed reveal) → Comparables & market → Director (statement, bio, marketing) → Cast → Crew → Watch (trailer slot) → The End.
